## What AirVeil does

AirVeil uses compatible AirPods head motion to place a blur or opaque cover over selected displays. Optional camera assistance helps establish screen direction and check whether you are still seated after headphone motion stops. Settings determine which features run.

If automatic display management, camera assistance, and seated dimming are all enabled, a usable seat reference lets AirVeil lower supported display brightness while it detects you seated and request display sleep after it detects that you have left. An unavailable or uncertain seat check does not authorize display sleep in that mode.

If automatic display management remains enabled while camera assistance or seated dimming is off, sustained motion loss can request display sleep without a camera check. Turning off camera assistance alone does not turn off automatic display management. You can disable automatic display management separately in Settings.

## Know the limits

AirVeil is a visual convenience, not a guarantee of confidentiality or a replacement for your Mac's screen lock. Blur can leave recognizable detail, and an overlay affects everyone looking at the same display. Other software, recordings, remote sessions, or external capture devices may see content differently. Check the behavior on your own hardware before relying on it.

The both-AirPods workflow uses an interruption in headphone motion as a signal for the enabled display-management mode. macOS does not provide AirVeil with proof that both earbuds are out; a sustained connection interruption can produce the same signal. A brief gap or a switch between tracking earbuds should not start a removal check. Motion resuming can restore brightness even if only one earbud has been put back in.

Camera results depend on lighting, camera availability, face visibility, and your saved screen setup. Detection can fail. Brightness control depends on display support. Keep the Mac's own brightness controls and normal wake method available. Pause & clear screen is available from AirVeil's menu and, when registered successfully, Control-Option-Command-P.

Display sleep follows your macOS Lock Screen password setting. AirVeil does not set a password policy or independently guarantee a lock. If you want a password immediately on waking, configure that in macOS. Do not rely on AirVeil for safety-critical monitoring, unattended access control, or protection against unauthorized software.
